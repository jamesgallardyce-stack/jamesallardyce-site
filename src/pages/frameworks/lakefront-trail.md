## Summary



The Lakefront Trail should function as a continuous, citywide spine for walking, running, and cycling that is safe enough for daily commuting and comfortable enough for recreation. The core objective is to reduce conflict between modes, eliminate ambiguous crossings, and provide predictable access points to beaches, parks, harbors, and adjacent neighborhoods. The trail should be designed as transportation infrastructure and public space, not as an informal path that users must negotiate.



## What the trail must do well



- **Continuity:** no “pinch points” that force sudden merges or weaving. ie. Waveland Tennis court weaving between bike path and pedestrian path with awkward painted roundabout by unnecessary car parking and the intersection by the Montrose bridgeway. 

- **Mode clarity:** people walking and cycling should not be forced into the same space in high-volume segments.

- **Safe crossings:** intersections and driveways should not depend on courtesy or reaction time.

- **Access:** people should be able to reach the lakefront from neighborhoods without unsafe gaps or detours.

- **Event readiness:** the trail should handle marathons, triathlons, festivals, and peak summer weekends without collapsing into conflict.



## Standard design moves



### 1) Separate high-volume walking and cycling where demand is high

In the busiest segments, a single mixed path produces predictable conflicts. The preferred approach is a parallel facility:

- one path optimized for walking/running and lingering

- one path optimized for cycling and commuting



### 2) Eliminate ambiguous crossings

Where the trail meets roads or access drives, the design should make priority obvious and self-enforcing:

- raised crossings (table) to slow vehicles

- tight turning geometry

- clear sightlines and lighting

- minimize the number of conflict points by consolidating access



### 3) Make access points legible

People should not need to “invent” crossing points. Access should be:

- frequent enough to be practical

- designed to concentrate crossings where drivers expect them

- compatible with beach/harbor access and maintenance operations



### 4) Design for universal access

The lakefront is a civic asset and must be navigable for:

- older adults

- mobility devices and electric wheelchairs

- strollers

- visitors unfamiliar with the area  

That means smooth surfaces, continuous curb ramps, and predictable crossing geometry.



## Quick-build vs permanent



**Quick-build (this season):**

- paint/markings to clarify walking vs cycling zones

- temporary separators in conflict segments

- access consolidation with signage and barriers

- interim raised crossing treatments where feasible



**Permanent (next year and beyond):**

- physically separated parallel paths in high-demand segments

- hardened raised crossings at access drives

- redesigned nodes near beaches/harbors for conflict reduction

- lighting and wayfinding upgrades



## Principle



The Lakefront Trail is one of Chicago’s most valuable public corridors. It should not require negotiation, conflict, or luck to use safely. A clear, separated, predictable design improves everyday mobility, recreation, tourism quality, and event operations.
