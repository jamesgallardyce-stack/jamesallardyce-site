\## Summary



Transit succeeds when it is reliable. The core tools are simple: dedicated space where delay is chronic, intersection priority where signals dominate travel time, and stop design that reduces dwell without sacrificing access.



\## What makes buses slow



\- Delay at signals and turning conflicts

\- Curbside friction (double parking, deliveries, ride-hail)

\- Excessive stop frequency or poor stop placement

\- Unreliable travel time that forces schedule padding



\## Core design moves



\- \*\*Bus lanes where delay is predictable\*\*

\- \*\*Signal priority\*\* (early green or green extension when a bus approaches)

\- \*\*Stop spacing discipline\*\* (fewer, higher-value stops where demand allows)

\- \*\*Stop design\*\* (bus bulbs, predictable curb access, clear boarding zones)

\- \*\*Curb management\*\* (loading zones and rules that keep buses out of general traffic)



\## Phasing



\- \*\*Quick-build:\*\* paint + posts, curb rules, signal timing changes

\- \*\*Permanent:\*\* hardened separators, bus bulbs, redesigned intersections, durable materials
