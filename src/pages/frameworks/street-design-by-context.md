\## Summary



Most streets can be designed using one of three standard configurations. The goal is not to force every corridor into the same template, but to make the street’s purpose legible: each design sets a target speed, establishes clear priority, and reduces conflict by allocating space based on context. These three options provide a consistent vocabulary for Chicago street design and can be applied corridor-by-corridor. Overall, trees are shifted towards the center of the street to give pedestrians better sidewalks and bike lanes give cyclists better conditions while leaving room for emergency vehicles to travel in the middle of the road. Neighborhood streets follow these same trends with a focus on bike priority with street parking for service vehicles. Note: Wide Streets have the greatest opportunity for roundabout intersections.
