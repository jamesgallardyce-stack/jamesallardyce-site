## Summary

Every mode has tradeoffs and no system is free. The issue is that the opportunity cost of car-first infrastructure is unusually high: it consumes land, increases exposure to severe injury, and imposes daily barriers on people who are not driving—especially older adults and people with mobility constraints.

## Who pays the cost

When streets are designed around vehicle speed and throughput, the cost is paid in longer crossings, missing curb access, higher crash severity, and reduced independence. People using mobility devices preserve independence when sidewalks are continuous, crossings are safe and frequent, and transit access is reliable.

## Why urgency matters

Some interventions can seem counterintuitive at first, especially when they challenge familiar driving habits. But we cannot afford to spend years waiting for consensus while preventable injuries and deaths continue. Evidence-based street design can save lives now, and education can follow implementation—not the other way around.
