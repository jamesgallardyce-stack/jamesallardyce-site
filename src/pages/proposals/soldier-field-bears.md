## Summary

Soldier Field and the surrounding lakefront should remain a premier civic destination designed first for pedestrians, cyclists, and public space. The area’s long-term value to Chicago is not primarily as a vehicle-access venue, but as a continuous waterfront park system that supports recreation, tourism, and daily public life. Any decisions involving the Bears should strengthen this role rather than expand highway capacity or surface parking.

## The lakefront is the asset

The land around Soldier Field is among the most valuable and irreplaceable public spaces in Chicago. Its best use is as a connected network of parks, paths, museums, and shoreline access that residents and visitors can use every day—not only on event days.

## Why Lake Shore Drive and parking lots are a bad deal

Lake Shore Drive and large surface parking lots impose a high opportunity cost on the city. They consume land that could support recreation and connectivity, create barriers between neighborhoods and the waterfront, and degrade the pedestrian experience with speed, noise, and wide crossing distances. They also reinforce event-day congestion patterns that spill into surrounding streets and require substantial ongoing maintenance for comparatively low civic value.

Adding more parking or expanding road access around Soldier Field does not solve mobility; it entrenches a car-first pattern on land that should function as a public destination.

## What mobility improvements should look like

If anything is added for mobility in this district, it should be transit-oriented and people-scaled:

- **Event-ready transit capacity:** improved bus operations and rail connectivity on game and concert days
- **Reliable transit priority:** dedicated transit lanes and signal priority where buses are delayed
- **Direct pedestrian access:** clear, legible walking routes from downtown, transit stations, and nearby neighborhoods
- **High-quality bicycle access:** continuous protected connections and safe crossings that reduce conflict
- **Managed curb space:** designated pick-up/drop-off zones that prevent chaotic stopping and double-parking

The goal is to move more people with fewer vehicles while improving safety and the lakefront experience.

## Implementation approach

- **Near-term:** improve event-day transit operations, add clear wayfinding, and manage curb space to reduce chaos
- **Mid-term:** expand pedestrian and bike priority connections and reduce the footprint of vehicle storage where feasible
- **Long-term:** align venue and site planning with a lakefront designed as a continuous park system, not a traffic facility

## Bottom line

The Museum Campus and Soldier Field district should function as a lakefront destination that is comfortable to walk and bike every day. Event operations should be planned around transit and safe access, not around expanding the footprint of roads and parking. Chicago’s lakefront is too valuable to be designed as a traffic facility.
